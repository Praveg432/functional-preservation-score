# Cover Letter

Dear Editors,

We submit **"Compression Works, Migration Does Not: An In-Silico Audit of Connectome-Based
Mind-Migration Claims Using a Functional Preservation Score"** for consideration.

**What the paper does.** Speculative "mind migration" proposals chain together optimistic
readings of real results in connectomics, graph ML, memory prosthetics, and neural interfaces.
Rather than argue about the vision, we convert its computational core into a falsifiable,
reproducible measurement problem and test it on public data runnable on a single laptop. We
introduce a Functional Preservation Score (FPS) and run five pre-registered studies.

**Why it matters.** The paper draws a clean, evidence-backed line: neural-data *compression* is
real and characterizable (a quantization sparsity floor on connectomes; ~10³× compressibility of
replay sequences with a bounded-store capacity law), whereas *migration of function across
substrates* is unsupported by the proposed partition→align→write pathway, which we show fails
in-silico at each stage for independent reasons, with the target substrate's wiring as the
recurring binding limit. For a field prone to over-claiming, a precise account of where and why
the idea breaks — plus a released measurement toolkit — is the contribution.

**Positioning and honesty.** We are explicit that all results are at the scale of a 306-neuron
nematode connectome and toy networks, and that the biological prerequisites (non-destructive
synaptic-resolution reading, identity-scale writing, cross-subject transfer) remain absent. We do
not claim progress toward human mind migration; the studies are coherence-in-simulation tests.
We also document the methodological episodes in which our own early results were corrected or
dropped, because that discipline is part of the contribution.

**Reproducibility.** Every quantitative claim maps to a committed result artifact and the script
that produced it; the metric, loaders, and all operations are released. No human subjects,
wet-lab, organoid, or cloning work is involved; all data are public animal datasets.

**Suggested venue fit.** The work sits at the intersection of computational neuroscience,
machine learning (graph compression, optimal transport, plasticity), and research methodology /
meta-science. It is suitable for a venue that values negative and corrective results and
reproducible, laptop-scale science.

We confirm the work is original, not under review elsewhere, and declare no competing interests.

Sincerely,
The Authors
