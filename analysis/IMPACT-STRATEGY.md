# Impact Strategy — a realistic but stubborn path to the goal

This is the document that refuses to give up on the goal while refusing to lie about it. The
five tracks are good papers; this is how they add up to *moving the field toward the actual
objective* instead of five disconnected results.

Stance: **be stubborn about the destination, ruthless about the route.** We do not get there
by pretending the blocked steps are solved. We get there by finding the one honest lever that,
if moved, changes what everyone downstream can do — and by building assets that compound.

---

## 1. Restate the goal at its defensible core

The blueprint's goal ("non-destructive human mind migration into a clone") is not a research
objective — it is a destination with ~4 absent prerequisites. Restated as something we can
actually push on without self-deception:

> **Substrate-independent persistence of a mind: the ability to represent, verify, and
> re-instantiate the function of a specific neural system in a different physical substrate
> with measurable fidelity.**

This reframing matters because it converts an unfalsifiable dream into a **measurable fidelity
problem** — and fidelity problems have gradients you can climb. Every track already measures a
fidelity. The strategy is to make those fidelities *the currency of the field*.

---

## 2. Find the real bottleneck (it is not what the blueprint thinks)

The blueprint spends its energy on the *write* and the *clone*. But the true rate-limiting
step for the whole program is upstream and unglamorous:

> **We have no agreed way to measure whether a mind/circuit has been preserved across a
> transformation.** There is no "fidelity metric for a transferred neural function."

This is the lever. If you cannot measure preservation, you cannot optimize it, certify it, or
even argue about it. Compression, alignment, and writing are all *optimizing against a metric
that does not yet exist in a standard form*. Everyone in this space hand-waves "the memory was
preserved." Nobody has a benchmark.

**Keystone bet #1 (the highest-impact thing in this whole program):**
Define and standardize a **Functional Preservation Score (FPS)** — a substrate-agnostic,
behaviorally-grounded metric for "how much of system A's input→output function survives
operation X (compress / align / transport / install)." T1–T5 each produce a slice of it; the
strategic move is to *unify them into one metric and release it as a benchmark others adopt.*

If FPS gets adopted, we stop being people who wrote five papers and become the people who
defined how the field keeps score. That is disproportionate, compounding impact — and it is
fully achievable on an M4 with public data.

---

## 3. The compounding-asset flywheel

Papers decay; assets compound. Each track is instructed to leave behind an asset the next
researcher *must* use:

```
   public datasets ──► [FPS metric + benchmark] ◄── every track scores against it
         │                      │
         ▼                      ▼
   worm→fly→mouse          leaderboard / released code
   validity ladder              │
         │                      ▼
         └──────────► "connectome operation" library ──► outside groups build on it
                      (compress, align, transport, install — all FPS-scored)
```

- **Asset 1 — FPS benchmark** (keystone). The scoreboard.
- **Asset 2 — a released "connectome operations" library** (compress / align / transport /
  install), each operation FPS-scored, each runnable on a laptop. Low barrier = adoption.
- **Asset 3 — the worm→fly→mouse validity ladder.** A result that climbs the ladder is
  believed; this becomes the standard way to argue generalization in connectome-function work.

The flywheel: datasets feed the benchmark → the benchmark makes operations comparable → the
operations library attracts users → users generate results on the ladder → results sharpen the
benchmark. Three turns of this and the sub-field organizes around our definitions.

---

## 4. Keystone bets (ranked by impact-per-unit-risk)

1. **FPS metric + benchmark** (above). Highest leverage, lowest compute, highest adoption
   potential. Do this *first*, threaded through T1–T5, not as an afterthought.
2. **The in-silico migration pipeline on C. elegans, end to end.** Compose T5→T2→T4 into a
   single runnable demonstration: partition a worm, align to another worm, transport+install a
   reflex-level function, score with FPS. Even if fidelity is low, *the first fully-measured,
   fully-reproducible in-silico "migration" of a defined function between two real nervous
   systems* is a landmark artifact — the honest, scaled-down version of the entire blueprint.
3. **An impossibility/limit theorem.** If T4/T2/T5 keep hitting walls (likely), consolidate
   them into a *formal statement of what local-rule, topology-constrained functional transport
   cannot do.* A clean limit result is more durable than a marginal positive — it tells the
   whole field where NOT to spend a decade.
4. **A "fidelity-gap" research agenda.** Publish the map of where every operation loses
   function, as an explicit to-do list for the community. Agenda-setting papers are cited for
   years and attract collaborators who extend your assets.

---

## 5. How to maximize real impact (operating principles)

- **Own a definition, not just a result.** Results get superseded; definitions (FPS, the
  ladder, the operations taxonomy) get adopted and outlive you.
- **Make the barrier to building on us near-zero.** Laptop-runnable, public data, released
  code, pinned environments. Adoption is impact; friction kills it.
- **Bank negative results as first-class.** In a field this speculative, a rigorous "this
  doesn't work and here's the boundary" is scarcer and more valuable than another optimistic
  demo. Our vetting-gate discipline already produces these — publish them, don't bury them.
- **Climb the validity ladder deliberately.** Worm (full, ground-truth) → fly sub-circuit →
  mouse column. A claim that survives all three is believed; one that only works on toys is not.
- **Stay one honest sentence away from the dream.** Every artifact carries the scope
  disclaimer *and* the line to the goal: "this is the measured, in-silico, animal-scale analogue
  of step X in substrate-independent mind persistence." That line is what makes a worm result
  matter to the big question without overclaiming.
- **Collaborate outward at the asset layer.** Wet-lab (organoid/MEA), connectomics, and OT-
  theory groups each own a piece we cannot build on a laptop. Our FPS + operations library is
  the natural interface to hand them — we become the measurement layer they plug into.

---

## 6. The decade framing (stubborn, not delusional)

The end goal is plausibly a multi-decade, multi-group effort gated by biology we cannot
accelerate from a laptop (the Tier-A reads/writes). What a laptop-scale program *can* do is own
the **measurement and methodology substrate** that any future biological breakthrough will have
to be evaluated against.

- **Years 0–1 (this program):** FPS metric + benchmark; T1/T3 core results; T5→T2→T4 surrogate
  chain; the end-to-end worm demo. Deliverable: the field's scoreboard + the first measured
  in-silico functional migration + a limit map.
- **Years 1–3 (extensions, some needing collaborators):** climb the ladder to mouse-cortex
  function; plug FPS into organoid/MEA closed-loop data from partner labs; harden the limit
  results into theory.
- **Years 3+ (gated by external biology):** when/if non-destructive high-resolution reads or
  plausible write modalities mature elsewhere, *our metric and operations library are the
  ready-made evaluation harness* — the program positioned exactly where the leverage lands.

**The stubborn bet:** we may never personally build the clone or the write. But if the field
one day can, it will keep score with a metric we defined, evaluate transport with operations we
released, and argue generalization on a ladder we standardized. That is how a laptop-scale,
honest program causes real impact on an audacious goal — by owning the layer that outlasts any
single breakthrough.

---

## 7. Kill-switch for the strategy itself

Stubborn is not the same as sunk-cost. Re-evaluate the whole program if:
- FPS shows **no** operation preserves function above chance on *any* substrate (the idea space
  is empty, not just hard), **or**
- the C. elegans end-to-end demo cannot exceed a random-transport baseline after honest effort
  (functional transport is not merely limited but absent even in the simplest real system).

Either outcome is still a publishable, field-shaping negative — but it would mean the honest
move is to redirect, not to keep pushing. We hold the destination stubbornly; we hold the route
only as long as the measurements say it leads somewhere.
